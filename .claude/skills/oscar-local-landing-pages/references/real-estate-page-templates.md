# Real Estate Local Pages — Deeper Structure

Applies specifically to Home Buyers / Oscar's Real Estate Team (and any future real estate site). Real estate local pages go deeper than the flat city×topic pattern other sites use — this structure was reverse-engineered from a real, ~100K-monthly-visitor competitor site (rubyhome.com) on Aug 23 2026. It is a STRUCTURE reference only — never copy actual competitor text; every fact on a real page must be freshly researched and written.

## The real hierarchy: 4 tiers, not 2

State → County → City → Community/Neighborhood

This is deeper than it first looks. A "city page" alone isn't enough — the real page-count and ranking power comes from the neighborhood tier underneath each city, where individual named subdivisions/developments each get their own page.

## Two content pillars, not one

1. **The geographic tree** above (State→County→City→Neighborhood) — this is the main structure.
2. **A separate cross-cutting Style/Property-Type pillar** (e.g. a "Styles" hub page listing architectural styles + property types like Condos, Gated Homes, Luxury Homes, Beachfront Homes — each cross-referenced against every city). This produces hundreds of additional narrow, exact-match pages (e.g. "Beverly Hills Gated Homes") separate from the neighborhood tree. Don't forget this pillar when planning a market — it's easy to build only the geographic tree and miss this whole second content type.

## City hub page template

1. Live MLS search results widget up top (auto-updating, "several times per day" — this is the IDX feed, not hand-written content)
2. Real estate market statistics snapshot (listed count, avg days on market, avg $/sqft, median list price)
3. Rich narrative sections: Community & Schools, Parks & Recreation, Arts & Entertainment, a "Why buy here" market overview
4. "Popular [City] Area Neighborhoods" link block near the bottom — a simple list of neighborhood names, each linking to its own page. This unglamorous block is the real engine of neighborhood-page discoverability; don't skip it as an afterthought.
5. Agent CTA

## Neighborhood/community page template

1. Video at the top
2. Sibling-neighborhood sub-navigation (e.g. "Neighborhoods in [District Name]" — note some markets have a DISTRICT tier between city and neighborhood, e.g. multiple neighborhoods grouped under a shared district name)
3. Short market-intro paragraph — why buy here specifically
4. **History section** — who developed it, when, why it looks the way it does today
5. **Architecture section** — the actual home styles present, named specifically (not "various styles" — real style names: Spanish Colonial, Mid-Century Modern, Craftsman, Ranch, etc.)
6. **Notable Homes** — specific named/addressed properties with real distinguishing facts (architect, historic designation, pop-culture connection)
7. **Notable Residents** — past and present, a genuine differentiator for shareability and local color
8. Agent CTA close

The History → Architecture → Notable Homes → Notable Residents sequence is the actual content moat. This is what a bare MLS feed can never produce on its own, and it's the part worth investing real research time into for every neighborhood.

## Internal + external linking pattern (a real trust signal, do this deliberately)

Every rich page links two ways: **internally** to sibling neighborhood/city pages (keeps visitors on-site, spreads link equity), and **externally** to real authority sources — city government department pages, Wikipedia articles on local landmarks/history, that kind of thing. Per the core skill's rule: external links must be fixed, pre-verified real URLs, never AI-guessed.

## The seller-side funnel (a two-stage valuation pattern worth replicating)

1. Free instant automated home-value estimate — low friction, address in, number out
2. Immediately followed by messaging that frames the free number as incomplete ("doesn't capture upgrades, condition, or unique features")
3. The real lead-capture step: a "Comprehensive" or human-reviewed valuation, positioned as the accurate one — this is where contact info actually gets collected, framed as a value-add rather than a bare form

Plus: city-specific "Sell a [City] Home" pages, parallel to the buyer-side city pages — don't forget the seller side when planning a market's page inventory.

## The IDX/enrichment split (confirmed architecture decision, Aug 23 2026)

The IDX/MLS feed integration generates the page SKELETON automatically and for free — every real subdivision/development name that exists in MLS data gets a page with live listings, no manual page-creation needed once that integration is live. The actual work — and the actual competitive moat — is the RESEARCH/ENRICHMENT layer described above (history, architecture, notable homes, notable residents) that gets built ON TOP of that auto-generated skeleton. Don't try to hand-build hundreds of neighborhood pages from scratch; build the enrichment content generator instead, designed to take a {city, neighborhoodName} pair as input and produce that content block, ready to slot onto whatever page the IDX feed creates.
