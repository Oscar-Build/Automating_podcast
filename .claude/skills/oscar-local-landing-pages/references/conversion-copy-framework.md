# Conversion Copy Framework (the persuasion layer, separate from SEO structure)

This is a different layer from the rest of this skill. `real-estate-page-templates.md` and the main SKILL.md cover what makes a page RANK (SEO structure, internal/external links, content depth). This file covers what makes a visitor who's already ON the page actually ACT — the persuasion sequence. Source: analysis of ClickFunnels' own landing pages, which reportedly generated $265M+ in a single year using this exact structure.

## When this applies

Use this framework for high-intent conversion pages — webinar signup pages, VSL (video sales letter) landing pages, ambassador/recruiting pages, "get a free consultation" pages, anything where the entire point of the page is one specific action. It applies LESS to a pure organic city/topic SEO page (those are optimized for search discovery first), but even those benefit from stronger headlines, social proof, and a clear FAQ section pulled from this pattern.

## Step zero: know the page's ONE job

Before writing anything, answer: is this page meant to generate a LEAD, drive a DIRECT SALE, or get someone into a FREE TRIAL/consultation? Everything below flows from this answer. A page trying to do all three at once does none of them well.

## The proven sequence

1. **Eyebrow text** — a short line ABOVE the headline that pre-qualifies the visitor or asks a question ("Struggling to get a steady flow of customers?"). Its job is to make the right person think "yes, that's me" before they even reach the headline.

2. **Headline** — must promise a dream outcome or ask a pointed question. Not a feature description. "Launch your next funnel in minutes" beats "AI Funnel Builder Software."

3. **Subheadline** — simple, straightforward support for the headline.

4. **First CTA** — clear, specific action language ("Create My Funnel with AI," not "Submit"). Often paired with a single field (email) to keep friction minimal.

5. **Social proof — immediately, above or just below the fold.** Don't wait. People decide fast, and seeing "1,000 funnels built last week" or real reviews right away shortcuts their decision-making. Social proof should appear in MORE THAN ONE place on the page, not just once.

6. **Problem section** — name the specific pain points the visitor is likely facing. Doesn't need to be long; even a headline + subheadline naming the problem works if the traffic is already warm. Follow immediately with another CTA — anyone convinced by the problem section is ready to act right now, don't make them scroll to find how.

7. **Solution section** — introduce the product/service as the answer to the named problems, directly.

8. **Value stacking** — list out the deliverables one by one, each with its own short description (and often its own mini-CTA). The psychology: the visitor knows they'll have to pay something, so the job of this section is to make the value clearly exceed the price before they ever see a number. Each stacked item should map to a specific pain point named earlier — don't just list generic features.

9. **Crossroad close** — a re-engagement moment for visitors who've scrolled this far but are still hesitating ("What are you waiting for?"). This is a deliberate checkpoint to pull doubters back before they leave, not filler.

10. **Social proof again** — reviews, testimonials, or an exclusivity angle ("could you be the next [award] winner") right after the crossroad close.

11. **Extended solution / deeper value stacking** — a second, more detailed pass at value stacking for visitors who need more convincing than the first pass gave them. Good use of imagery/graphics here — consistent, professional branding builds trust; inconsistent or low-quality graphics reads as untrustworthy no matter how good the copy is.

12. **FAQ — 8 to 15 real questions, accordion format.** This is where remaining objections get handled directly. Don't skip real objections to make the product look better; addressing them head-on is what removes the final hesitation.

13. **Final CTA** — the last ask.

## Core principles that apply throughout

- **CTAs need to be frequently accessible.** A visitor ready to act shouldn't have to scroll far to find the button — place a CTA after every major section, not just at the top and bottom.
- **People buy with emotion and justify with logic.** Comparison framings ("your business with this vs. without it") work because they trigger a real emotional response (fear of missing out, relief), not because they're the most logically rigorous argument. Don't over-intellectualize the copy at the expense of emotional resonance.
- **Address objections explicitly rather than hoping they don't come up.** If a visitor's real hesitation is "is this too technical for me," say so directly and answer it — don't just avoid the topic.
- **Graphics and branding consistency matters more than it seems.** Visitors expect the landing page to look like the ad/social presence that brought them there. A mismatch reads as untrustworthy or scammy even if the copy itself is solid.

## Applying this to Oscar's actual pages

- **EDP webinar/seminar signup pages** — a natural fit for the full sequence: eyebrow (a Medi-Cal or probate fear question) → headline (protect your family/home) → problem (what happens without a trust) → solution → value stack (what the seminar covers) → FAQ (real UPL-safe objections) → final CTA. Keep every claim compliance-checked against the edp-compliance skill — this framework is about structure and psychology, not license to make claims EDP can't legally make.
- **Real estate valuation/lead pages (Home Buyers, CHVO, SMHFTM)** — the two-stage valuation funnel documented in `real-estate-page-templates.md` IS this pattern already, just specific to real estate: free instant tool (low-friction first CTA) → "but it's incomplete" (problem framing) → comprehensive valuation (the real value-stacked offer) → contact form (final CTA).
- **Ambassador/ambassador-recruiting pages (EDP)** — value stacking applies directly to "what you get as an ambassador," but every compensation-related claim must stay compliance-safe (no dollar figures/guaranteed income per existing EDP rules) — the persuasion structure doesn't override compliance limits.
- **Pure city/topic SEO pages** — don't force the full 13-step sequence onto these; they're built for organic search discovery first. But borrow the cheap wins: a stronger dream-outcome-style headline instead of a flat "Homes for Sale in [City]," social proof somewhere on the page if genuine social proof exists, and a real FAQ section (which already overlaps with the SEO requirement for FAQs in the main skill).
